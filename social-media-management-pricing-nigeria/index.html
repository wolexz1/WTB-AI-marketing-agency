Short answer

Cheap posting is not the same thing as social media marketing.

Posting alone keeps a page active. Social media marketing connects content to the brand offer, customer psychology, campaign timing, ads, creators, and reporting. That is why pricing changes based on the scope.

If your brand needs strategy, UGC, influencers, ads, video scripts, and reporting, the budget should be higher than basic caption posting.

What affects price

What changes the cost of social media management?

Strategy

Content direction

Audience research, brand voice, offer angles, platform direction, campaign themes, and posting structure.

Production

Content volume

More posts, reels, carousels, scripts, product demos, and UGC assets require more planning and production time.

Campaigns

Paid social ads

Meta, TikTok, X, LinkedIn, YouTube, and Snapchat ads need audience setup, creative testing, retargeting, and reporting.

Creators

Influencer and UGC support

Creator discovery, briefs, negotiation support, posting calendars, approval, and performance tracking add work.

Reporting

Performance learning

Serious brands need monthly or weekly reports that show what to repeat, stop, improve, and test next.

Growth

Conversion support

Landing pages, WhatsApp follow-up, email/SMS capture, and offer testing help turn attention into action.

WTB guidance

How to choose the right social media budget.

For basic visibility

Start with a content calendar, captions, design direction, and consistent posting. This works when the brand already has clear offers and assets.

For growth campaigns

Add paid social ads, influencer support, UGC, and reporting. This is better when you need leads, sales, or campaign momentum.

For a complete system

Combine social media with SEO, Google Ads, email/SMS marketing, website improvements, automation, and reporting.
