AI activities for kids at home: seven safe ways to learn together.

By WTB Team | Updated July 23, 2026

The best early AI activities are not about making a child use every new app. They are about helping them notice patterns, ask better questions, protect privacy and keep their own imagination in the driver's seat.

Explore AI Explorers for ages 9-11
Short answer

Choose small activities that make children think, explain and create with a parent nearby; avoid activities that turn AI into an answer machine.

Seven simple activities to try

  1. Spot the pattern: look at music, video or shopping recommendations and discuss why a system may have made that suggestion.
  2. Fact-check a fun answer: ask a chatbot a harmless question, then verify one claim using a book or trusted website.
  3. Build a family quiz: use AI to suggest question ideas, then let the child choose, improve and write the final quiz.
  4. Make a story plan: ask for three story starters, then have the child invent the characters and ending.
  5. Privacy detective: sort example details into safe-to-share and keep-private piles.
  6. Improve a weak prompt: compare a vague question with a clear question and notice the difference in the response.
  7. Create a helpful poster: make a poster of family AI rules using the child's own words and drawings.

What makes an activity safe?

A good activity has an adult nearby, does not require personal data, leaves room for the child's judgement, and ends with a short conversation. If the tool starts doing all the work, the activity has missed the point.

Keep the standard realistic

You do not need special equipment or hours of preparation. A phone, tablet, printed page or simple family discussion can be enough. Consistency beats complexity.
