App marketing and user acquisition

How to market an app in Nigeria: organic videos, UGC and paid ads.

By WTB Team | Updated July 27, 2026

Building the app is only the first half. The real challenge is giving the right people a reason to install, try the important feature and return.

Plan your app growth campaign
Best starting point

Use a three-phase system: create useful organic videos, turn the strongest ideas into authentic UGC, then use the best-performing content as the starting point for paid acquisition. Measure activation and retention—not only installs.

Downloads are not the finish line

An app can collect thousands of installs and still have no business. If the store page is unclear, onboarding is slow, the first value is hidden or the app solves a problem nobody urgently feels, more ads simply buy more disappointment.

Why app marketing is a real opportunity in Nigeria

Google reports more than 1.1 billion app installs across sub-Saharan Africa. Its app-marketing research also reports a 24% year-on-year increase in in-app purchase revenue in 2024 and a 28% rise in non-organic downloads across the region. These are regional signals, not a promise that every app will win, but they show that distribution and monetisation deserve deliberate strategy.

Source: Think with Google app-marketing lessons for South Africa and Nigeria.

The three-phase app growth plan

Phase 1: 10 useful videos

Show the problem, the before-and-after, one feature in context, a common mistake and a real use case. Teach first; pitch second.

Phase 2: 10 UGC creators

Give selected creators clear briefs and room to speak naturally. Let users see how the app fits into a real day, not just a polished product tour.

Phase 3: iterate with paid media

Use the strongest organic and UGC videos as creative hypotheses. Test hooks, audiences, stores, landing paths and post-install events before scaling.

Always: improve activation

Track whether a new user completes the first valuable action. An install without activation is a very expensive “hello”.

What your first 10 organic videos should answer

  1. What painful problem does the app solve?
  2. Who is it for—and who is it not for?
  3. What can a new user do in the first five minutes?
  4. What does the app replace or simplify?
  5. How does it work with Nigerian payment, language, data or workflow realities?
  6. What would a sceptical customer ask before installing?
  7. What mistake does the app help users avoid?
  8. What result can users reasonably expect?
  9. What does the founder understand about the user?
  10. What is the clearest next step: install, join a waitlist or book a demo?

Make the store page do its job

Your app-store listing is a sales page. The name, icon, screenshots, description, reviews and first visible promise must agree. Show the experience, not a list of vague adjectives. If the app helps a merchant track stock, show the stock decision. If it helps a team schedule work, show the moment a task becomes clearer.

Do not optimise for installs alone

Choose the event that represents real value: registration completed, first search, first booking, first transaction, first lesson or first team invite. Google recommends conversion tracking, relevant deep links, varied creative assets and a clear campaign goal for App campaigns. Read Google’s current App campaign best practices.

Useful dashboard

Track installs, activation rate, cost per activated user, day-7 retention, key in-app action, paid-versus-organic mix and revenue or qualified leads. If you only report CPI, you may celebrate buying people who never use the product.

Where UGC earns its place

UGC works when it reduces uncertainty. Let people see the app used by someone who resembles them, in a setting that feels believable, with a specific problem and a clear demonstration. Do not make ten creators repeat the same script. Give them ten useful angles and keep the best proof.

When to spend on ads

Paid media should amplify a message that has earned evidence, not rescue an app with weak onboarding. Before scaling, test the store page, event tracking, deep links, creative variations and support path. Google notes that app campaigns use a learning period and that drastic budget or bid changes can disrupt performance, so judge early data with conversion delay in mind.
