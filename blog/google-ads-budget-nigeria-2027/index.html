Google Ads Budget in Nigeria: How Much Should Your Business Spend in 2027?

By WTB Team | Published November 1, 2026

Plan a Google Ads budget in Nigeria using customer value, qualified leads, conversion rates and a realistic test.

Talk to WTBMore guides

“How much should I spend on Google Ads?” is a fair question. Unfortunately, “Just put ₦100,000 and watch the customers enter” is not a fair answer.

Your Google Ads budget depends on what you sell, what a customer is worth, the searches you target, competition, location, conversion rates and how well your business follows up. Two companies can spend the same amount and get completely different results. One has a clear offer and a working website. The other has a homepage that loads like it is travelling from Lagos to Abuja by bicycle.

Here is how to plan a sensible test for 2027 without confusing activity with profit. Figures below are illustrations, not forecasts or quoted market rates. Platform settings and taxes can change, so check the latest rules and your account before spending.

The short answer

There is no universal Google Ads budget that works for every Nigerian business. Start with the value of a sale, estimate what you can afford to pay to acquire one, work backwards to an affordable cost per qualified lead, and compare that with the likely cost of reaching enough searchers to learn. Use a small, controlled test and judge it on qualified leads and sales, not clicks alone.

Google's Keyword Planner can help estimate searches and costs, but its estimates are not promises. Google says billing information and completed account setup are required for some basic features.

Step 1: Know what a customer is worth

Before asking Google to find customers, ask your calculator what a customer is worth to you. Use contribution after the relevant costs of fulfilling the order, not just revenue.

For a simple illustration, say a service sells for ₦150,000 and the costs directly associated with delivering it are ₦90,000. That leaves ₦60,000 before advertising and other overheads. It does not mean you should spend all ₦60,000 acquiring one customer. You still need to allow for overheads, profit, unsuccessful leads and uncertainty.

Choose a maximum acquisition cost your business can genuinely sustain. If customers often return, you can consider repeat purchases, but use evidence from your own records rather than assuming everyone will become a loyal customer.

Step 2: Work backwards from sales to qualified leads

Suppose your business can afford ₦30,000 in advertising cost per sale, and historically one out of five qualified leads becomes a customer. Your indicative maximum cost per qualified lead would be:

₦30,000 × 20% = ₦6,000 per qualified lead.

This is only a planning example. It is not a recommended Nigerian benchmark, and a new business may not yet know its close rate. If you have no records, call the estimate a hypothesis and update it once you have enough real conversations.

A qualified lead is someone who fits your service, location and buying criteria, not simply everyone who typed “Hello” at midnight.

Step 3: Estimate the clicks needed

Now estimate how many website visitors or calls may turn into qualified enquiries. If your working assumption is that 5% of clicks become qualified leads, one qualified lead would require about 20 clicks on average. At an illustrative ₦300 per click, that would be about ₦6,000 per qualified lead.

20 clicks × ₦300 = ₦6,000.

Real results can differ substantially. Keyword prices fluctuate, clicks do not arrive evenly, and a 5% conversion rate may not fit your offer. Keyword Planner and your own account's search-term and conversion data are better inputs than a universal price list.

Step 4: Turn your monthly test amount into a daily budget

Google uses an average daily budget for many campaigns. For a campaign running throughout a typical month with a stable budget, Google explains the monthly charging limit using an average of 30.4 days. A ₦91,200 monthly illustration therefore works out to approximately ₦3,000 per day.

That is not a suggestion that ₦91,200 is right for your business. Google may spend more than the average daily amount on an individual day, potentially up to twice that average under its standard daily spending limit, while applying the relevant monthly charging limit. Budget changes, partial months and campaign-specific settings affect the calculation. Review your account's current spending limits and billing details, including applicable taxes and fees, before launching.

In other words, “₦3,000 daily” does not mean the dashboard must spend exactly ₦3,000 every single day. Google is not your neighbourhood contribution box.

Step 5: Put the budget where intent is clearest

For a first Search campaign, start with a specific service, a location you actually serve, and a page that answers the buyer's question. Someone searching “emergency plumber Ikeja” may be at a different buying stage from someone researching “how plumbing works.”

Make the ad and landing page agree. If the ad offers a consultation, the page should make the consultation easy to understand and request. Check the search terms that trigger ads, add appropriate negative keywords, and review location settings. Do not assume every automated recommendation is suitable for your campaign.

Step 6: Measure what happens after the click

Track the path from ad to enquiry and from enquiry to sale. Depending on your setup, useful signals include form submissions, calls, qualified conversations, booked appointments, sales and revenue. A WhatsApp button click can be useful, but it does not prove that a conversation or sale happened.

Agree with the sales team on what “qualified” means. Then compare:

  • Spend and clicks.
  • Enquiries and qualified leads.
  • Cost per qualified lead.
  • Booked appointments or orders.
  • Sales value and, where possible, profit after advertising costs.

If the leads are relevant but nobody follows up, the problem is not necessarily that Google needs more money. Your ad has delivered a guest to the door. Somebody still needs to open it.

Step 7: Test before scaling

Start with a clear offer, a working page, conversion tracking and a realistic budget you can afford to learn with. Review results over a meaningful period, but don't declare a winning or losing strategy from a handful of clicks. Small samples are noisy.

If the numbers are not viable, investigate search intent, offer clarity, location, page speed, conversion tracking and follow-up before simply increasing the budget. More fuel does not repair a leaking tank.

FAQs

Is there a fixed Google Ads cost per click in Nigeria?

No. Cost varies by keyword, competition, quality, location and auction conditions. Use current estimates and your own campaign data; do not treat any one published naira figure as a promise.

Can I run Google Ads with a small budget?

Often, yes, but a budget must be large enough for the relevant clicks and the learning you need. Narrow your focus and calculate what a realistic test would require before deciding whether to run ads now.

Does Google Ads management cost the same as ad spend?

No. The money paid to Google for media is separate from any agency management fee, creative, website work, taxes or other costs. Ask for a clear breakdown before agreeing to a campaign.
