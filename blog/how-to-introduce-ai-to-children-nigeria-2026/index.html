A first AI conversation

How to introduce AI to children in Nigeria: a calm parent guide.

By WTB Team | Updated July 23, 2026

You do not need a technical lecture. Start with a question, a small experiment and an adult who stays curious.

Start with AI Explorers
Best first step

Ask your child what they think AI is, then demonstrate one safe, useful task such as turning a story idea into questions. Do not begin by giving a child an unrestricted account.

Curiosity before complexity

Children do not need to know how a model is trained before they can understand that AI predicts and generates responses from patterns. They do need to know that a fluent answer is not automatically true, private or wise.

A five-step first lesson

  1. Ask what they already notice. “Where have you seen a recommendation, voice assistant or face filter?”
  2. Explain the tool plainly. “AI is software that finds patterns and produces an answer. It can help, but it can also make mistakes.”
  3. Choose a low-risk task. Create a quiz about animals, improve a fictional story opening or classify household objects.
  4. Check the output together. Ask what sounds wrong, what needs evidence and what information should never be shared.
  5. Close with a family rule. Use AI to learn and create; keep humans responsible for decisions.

Use familiar Nigerian examples

Market maths

Ask AI for a fictional budgeting puzzle, then let the child solve it and check the arithmetic with an adult.

Storytelling

Build a story set in Lagos, Kano or Enugu. The child chooses the characters, values and ending.

Language play

Compare a short English explanation with a familiar phrase, while discussing that translation can lose meaning.

Fact detective

Give the tool one deliberately tricky question and teach the child to verify its answer with a book or trusted site.

What not to share

Keep names, school details, home addresses, phone numbers, passwords, family conflicts, identifiable photos and private medical information out of prompts. If a task needs personal context, replace it with a fictional example.

How to keep the conversation open

Children may hide AI use if the first response is anger. Tell them they can show you an answer that feels strange, unsafe or embarrassing without being punished for asking. Review the tool together and focus on judgement rather than shame.

UNICEF’s updated guidance centres safety, privacy, transparency, fairness, inclusion and children’s development. Read the guidance.

A gentle bridge to structured learning

AI Explorers turns the first conversation into short missions for ages 5–11, so the child is not simply staring at a blank chat box. Each activity gives the parent a reason to ask, “What did you notice?” and “How do you know?”
