App marketing budget in Nigeria

How much does it cost to market an app in Nigeria? A practical ₦5 million launch plan.

By WTB Team | Updated August 22, 2026

Building the app can feel like the finish line. Then launch day arrives, and the app sits there quietly like a beautiful shop in a street nobody knows exists. Distribution is not an afterthought. It is the bridge between a working product and the people who need it.

Short answer

For a serious first test in Nigeria, a starting budget of about ₦5 million over 30 to 60 days can give an app enough room to produce creative, test UGC, compare Meta, TikTok and Google, and learn what happens after the install. It is not a promise of virality. It is a structured learning and distribution budget.

Why an app needs more than a launch post

Your app may be useful. That does not mean a stranger understands it in three seconds, trusts it enough to install, or reaches the first useful action after opening it.

App marketing has to answer four uncomfortable questions:

  • Who is this app for?
  • What problem does it solve today?
  • Why should someone trust it with their time, money or data?
  • What should the user do immediately after installing?

Paid media can buy attention, but it cannot repair a confusing promise or a broken onboarding journey. That is why distribution and product readiness must work together.

A practical ₦5 million app-marketing starting plan

Suggested 30-to-60-day test

Meta Ads: ₦1,000,000
TikTok Ads: ₦1,000,000
Google App Campaigns: ₦1,000,000
UGC creators and content testing: ₦1,200,000
Editing, store assets, analytics and iteration: ₦800,000

This allocation is a starting framework, not a sacred law. An app with strong search demand may deserve more Google budget. A visual consumer app may learn faster on TikTok. A broad product with strong retargeting potential may benefit from Meta. The data should earn the next naira.

Meta Ads: broad discovery and retargeting

Meta can help you reach people based on interests, behaviours and creative response, then bring back people who watched, clicked, visited or installed but did not complete the next action.

TikTok Ads: native attention and UGC testing

TikTok is useful when the app can be demonstrated quickly and naturally. The creative should feel like a person showing a useful discovery, not a committee announcing a product.

Google App Campaigns: intent and scale

Google can help connect an app to people searching, watching videos, browsing Discover or using Google Play, depending on campaign setup and market. It becomes more useful when conversion tracking tells the system what a valuable user actually does.

Why UGC should come before a full ad push

People want to see the app from the user's side. They want to know what happens when someone like them opens it, tries it, gets stuck, succeeds, or finally says, "Ah, this is actually useful."

WTB's recommended first phase is:

  1. Create 10 organic videos, each built around a real problem and a visible app outcome.
  2. Work with about 10 UGC creators who can show different user situations and voices.
  3. Publish the content consistently and compare watch time, saves, comments, clicks and quality of traffic.
  4. Turn the strongest organic and UGC videos into paid tests.
  5. Create variations of the winners instead of making one lucky video carry the whole campaign.

What should you measure besides installs?

A cheap install can be a very expensive victory if the user never opens the app again. Track the full path:

  • Ad view to click
  • Click to install
  • Install to account creation
  • Account creation to first valuable action
  • First action to trial, purchase, subscription or booking
  • Retention after 3, 7 and 30 days

The right success metric depends on the app. For a marketplace it may be a completed transaction. For a productivity app it may be a recurring workflow. For a finance app it may be a verified account or funded wallet. Define that event before spending heavily.

When ₦5 million is not the right budget

If the app is not stable, onboarding is confusing, the store listing is weak, tracking is missing, or the offer is still unclear, more ad spend will only help you discover the problem faster. In that case, spend first on product readiness, positioning, creative and measurement.

On the other hand, if the app has strong retention, a clear audience and a proven conversion event, ₦5 million may be only the first learning phase before scaling.
