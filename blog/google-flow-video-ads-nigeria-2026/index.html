Google Flow for Nigerian brands

Google Flow video ads in Nigeria: how to make clips that help sell.

By WTB Team | Published October 25, 2026

The camera sweeps across a spotless Lagos street. The lighting is beautiful. Your product is nowhere to be found. Your customer scrolls past, and your ad budget goes sightseeing. Let's make a video with a proper sales job instead.

Short answer

Yes, Nigerian brands can use Google Flow to develop video ad ideas. Start with the buyer and offer, use your real product as a reference where accuracy matters, review every generated frame, then test the finished ad against enquiries or sales. A fine clip on its own is not a campaign.

Can you use Google Flow in Nigeria?

Yes. Nigeria is on Google's supported-country list. Flow can make clips from text prompts, images and reference frames, although features can vary by country and account. Check your account before promising a client a particular feature. For the actual controls, use Google's current creation guide.

Do the marketing work before you write the prompt

Write down who should buy, what they want, what might stop them and where they will buy. Imagine a Lagos furniture shop selling a compact dining table. “Make a luxury apartment ad” is too loose. “Show how this exact table seats four in a small apartment, include its dimensions and finish, then direct buyers to our order page” gives the creative a job.

Now gather the real product photographs, price, delivery areas and anything you are allowed to claim. If the generated table has six legs or turns walnut into gold halfway through, do not run it. The buyer will not accept “the prompt was feeling creative” as a delivery explanation.

Give every part of the video a purpose

  1. Open with a recognisable situation. Show the cramped dining space or the frustration your service solves. Make it clear in the first few seconds who the ad is for.
  2. Show the offer honestly. Use your actual product images or footage when colour, size, packaging or fit matters. Flow can help develop a setting or motion, but it cannot be your witness that a result happened.
  3. Answer the buying questions. Say what is included, what it costs or the useful price range, where it is available and what happens after someone orders.
  4. End with one action. Send people to a working product page, booking form or WhatsApp conversation. Do not ask them to visit six places and solve a puzzle.

For service businesses, show the process and outcome without inventing a customer testimonial. A short human voiceover often carries more trust than a generated character speaking with an accent nobody in your office has ever heard.

Check the ad like a customer would

Watch it on a phone with sound off, then with sound on. Can you understand the offer either way? Check captions, product details, pronunciation, likenesses and claims. If a scene looks like real footage of your business but was generated, do not use it to mislead people. Google's generative AI policy also prohibits deceptive impersonation and misleading claims of provenance.

Measure orders, not just oohs and ahhs

Test two genuinely different openings against the same offer. Track useful actions such as qualified chats, bookings, add-to-carts or purchases. If people watch but never click, inspect the hook and offer. If they click and disappear, inspect the page, price and follow-up. Make the next edit based on the leak, not on which video your team likes most.
