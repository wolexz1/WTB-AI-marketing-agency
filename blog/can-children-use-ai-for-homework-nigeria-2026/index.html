AI literacy for Nigerian families

Can children use AI for homework? A Nigerian parent guide.

By WTB Team | Updated July 23, 2026

Yes—but the best use of AI is as a patient tutor, not a secret substitute for your child’s brain.

See the parent-guided AI Explorers workbook
Short answer

AI can explain a difficult idea, create practice questions and help a child review their reasoning. It should not write a submission the child cannot explain, verify or defend in their own words.

The real question is not “AI or no AI?”

It is “Who is still doing the thinking?” In a busy Nigerian home, homework already competes with traffic, chores, extra lessons, power interruptions and the famous 9 p.m. school-run panic. A helpful tool can reduce frustration. It must not turn learning into copy, paste and prayer.

Three healthy ways to use AI for schoolwork

1. Explain

Ask for a simpler explanation, an analogy or a worked example. Then ask your child to explain the idea back without looking.

2. Practise

Generate five questions at the child’s level. Hide the answers until the child has tried, then discuss the mistakes.

3. Review

Paste the child’s own draft and ask for questions about clarity or missing steps—not a replacement essay.

4. Reflect

Ask: “What did you already know? What changed? What will you try next time?” That is where the learning sticks.

Prompts parents can use tonight

  • “Explain fractions using a market-sharing example suitable for a 10-year-old.”
  • “Give my child three practice questions on this topic, one at a time, without revealing the answer.”
  • “Ask questions that help my child find the mistake in this solution.”
  • “Turn this topic into a five-minute oral quiz for a parent and child.”

When homework help becomes cheating

Pause when the child submits wording they do not understand, asks AI to complete every step, hides the use of the tool, or cannot reproduce the method without it. Do not make the conversation a courtroom drama. Ask the child to close the screen, explain the answer aloud and redo one similar question from scratch.

Protect privacy and accuracy

Do not enter a child’s full name, school, phone number, address, passwords, photographs or private family details into a chatbot. AI can sound confident and still be wrong, so check dates, calculations, quotations and scientific claims against the teacher’s notes or a trusted source. OpenAI says ChatGPT is not intended for children under 13; for younger children using it educationally, the direct interaction should be conducted by an adult. Check the current age guidance.

A family rule that is easy to remember

Try this

“Use AI to understand, practise and improve. Never use it to pretend you did work you cannot explain.”

This rule leaves room for curiosity while protecting effort, honesty and confidence. UNICEF’s current child-centred AI guidance also puts safety, privacy, transparency, inclusion and children’s development at the centre—not novelty for novelty’s sake.

Sources: UNICEF Guidance on AI and Children; UNESCO guidance for generative AI in education.
