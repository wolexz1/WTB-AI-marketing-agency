Game marketing in Nigeria: how to find players who actually come back.

By WTB Team | Published October 7, 2026

You built a game. Congratulations. Now comes the boss level: getting people to discover it, try it, tell a friend and return tomorrow. “We launched!” is exciting for the team; it is not yet a distribution strategy.

Short answer

To market a game in Nigeria, define the player and the promise, test a playable build with real people, make short gameplay proof, choose the right store and creator channels, then measure activated and returning players. A download is the door opening, not the victory screen.

First decide what kind of game you are selling

A mobile puzzle game, multiplayer football title, story-led PC game and esports tournament need different routes. Write one sentence: “This is a game for [player], who wants [experience], and the first 30 seconds prove it by [mechanic].” If that sentence is foggy, your trailer will be foggy too.

For Nigerian players, test the realities of your audience: device performance, download size, data use, connectivity, payment options and whether the humour or references travel. These are product questions to validate with players—not assumptions about every Nigerian gamer. An ad cannot rescue a game that fails on the phone your audience actually owns.

Before launch: recruit players, not just followers

Run small playtests with the people you want to reach. Watch where they stop, what they misunderstand and whether they ask to play again. Capture permission to contact interested testers. Show actual gameplay in short videos: a funny failure, surprising mechanic, satisfying win or creator reaction. The hook is the experience, not a cinematic logo reveal that costs more than the whole tutorial.

For Android launches, Google Play describes pre-registration and pre-launch testing as ways to build awareness and gather early feedback. For PC games, build a credible Steam page early and use wishlists as an interest signal; Steam says wishlists are not a guaranteed sales formula. Do not market a Steam wishlist to an Android-only audience.

Launch: give every channel a job

Store discovery

Use a title, icon, screenshots and preview that show the genre and actual play. Test store-listing creative rather than guessing. If your game is on both stores, build the correct path for each device. A beautiful ad that sends an Android user to the wrong destination is expensive comedy.

Creators and communities

Brief gaming creators who genuinely play the genre. Give them a build, the key mechanic and room to react honestly. Short-form clips on TikTok, Reels and YouTube Shorts can demonstrate the game quickly; X, Discord and gaming communities can deepen conversation when your audience is there. Creator fit, audience geography and measurable links matter more than follower count alone.

Paid distribution and PR

Use small creative tests before scaling Meta, TikTok, Google App campaigns or YouTube. Track which clip brings players who finish onboarding, not merely cheap installs. Pitch gaming and tech publications a real story—an original mechanic, local creative process or useful launch data—not “please post our app” sent to 100 editors.

After launch: retention is distribution too

Watch first-open to first-play, tutorial completion, day-one and day-seven return rates, referrals and revenue where relevant. Ask players why they quit. Fix confusing onboarding and weak early progression before buying another million impressions. New levels, events, creator challenges and thoughtful community updates give players a reason to return and talk about the game.

One practical 30-day plan: week one, interview testers and repair the first session; week two, publish gameplay clips and a store page; week three, test three creator angles and two paid creatives; week four, compare cost per activated player and retention, then put budget behind the strongest route. Your numbers decide the next move—not somebody shouting “viral” in the meeting.

FAQ

How do I promote a new mobile game in Nigeria?

Start with player testing and a clear gameplay hook, then improve the store listing, recruit relevant creators, test short videos and paid campaigns, and measure activated and returning players.

Do I need a huge influencer budget?

No. A small set of creators who actually play your genre and can show the game well may be a better first test than one expensive celebrity post.
