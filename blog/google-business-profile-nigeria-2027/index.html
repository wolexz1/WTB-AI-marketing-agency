Google Business Profile in Nigeria: How to Get Found on Google Maps in 2027

By WTB Team | Published November 1, 2026

Learn how eligible Nigerian businesses can improve their Google Business Profile and turn local searches into enquiries.

Talk to WTBMore guides

Your business has a brilliant service, happy customers, a WhatsApp number that never rests, and an Instagram page that looks like money. Lovely. But when someone nearby searches Google for what you sell, can they actually find you?

If the answer is no, your next customer may not even know they missed you. They have already called the business Google showed them first.

This guide explains how eligible Nigerian businesses can create or improve a Google Business Profile, appear accurately on Search and Maps, and turn local discovery into enquiries. It is based on Google's rules available in October 2026. Check the current rules again before making changes in 2027.

The short answer

A Google Business Profile is free to create and manage. For an eligible business, start with accurate business details, complete the verification Google offers, choose a relevant category, add useful photos and services, and encourage genuine reviews. Keep the phone number, hours and website up to date. None of this guarantees a top position: Google considers relevance, distance and popularity when choosing local results.

One important caveat: not every business qualifies. Google generally requires in-person contact with customers during stated hours, subject to exceptions. An online-only business should not invent a shop or rent an address just to appear on Maps.

Why this matters to a Nigerian business

Imagine a customer looking for a caterer in Ikeja, an auto repairer in Abuja or a beauty studio in Lekki. They may search Google, compare reviews, check opening hours, look at photographs and call without ever finding the business on Instagram.

A complete profile gives that person a way to understand and contact you. It is not a replacement for a good website, customer service or a strong offer. It is another doorway into the business. And a beautiful doorway still needs someone to answer when the customer knocks.

Step 1: Check whether your business is eligible

Businesses with a customer-facing location or businesses that travel to customers may qualify. Google's current eligibility guidance generally excludes online-only businesses and lead-generation companies. Read the rules for your own business model before proceeding.

If you visit customers rather than receive them at your address, follow Google's service-area guidance. Do not display a home address if customers are not served there. Avoid fake locations, post-office boxes and unsupported virtual offices. A coworking address is not automatically eligible.

The principle is simple: represent the business as it actually operates. Google Maps is not where we go to rent imaginary office space in every Nigerian state.

Step 2: Find or create your profile

Search for your business name on Google Maps first. You may already have a listing that needs to be claimed. If an eligible business does not already have one, create it through Google Business Profile.

Use the real-world business name, a primary category that accurately describes your main activity, your actual service area or eligible address, and contact details customers can use. Do not stuff the business name with keywords that are not part of the real name.

Choose categories for what the business is, rather than every service you hope to sell. A clear, accurate category gives Google and customers a better starting point.

Step 3: Complete the verification Google offers

Google decides which verification methods are available for a particular business. Follow the instructions shown in your account, keep your details consistent and allow time for review. Do not pay anyone promising a guaranteed verification shortcut.

If your profile already exists and someone else manages it, use Google's ownership-request process rather than creating a duplicate.

Step 4: Make the profile useful, not just present

Once your profile is verified, check the basics:

1. Contact details: Does the phone number work, and does someone respond to enquiries? 2. Hours: Are normal and holiday hours correct? 3. Website: Does the link lead to a fast, relevant page rather than a confusing dead end? 4. Services and products: Are the descriptions accurate and easy to understand? 5. Photos: Show your real location, team, products or work where appropriate and with permission. 6. Reviews: Ask genuine customers for honest feedback and respond professionally. Do not buy fake reviews or offer incentives for positive ones.

The objective is not to decorate the profile until it wins Miss Google Maps. It is to help the right customer make a confident next move.

Step 5: Build the journey after the search

A profile view is not the same as a sale. Decide what should happen next: a phone call, a website enquiry, directions to your location or another available contact option.

Then check the journey yourself on a mobile phone. Does the website load? Can a customer see what you offer? Is the phone answered? If the person reaches WhatsApp through your website, does somebody respond with useful information?

An excellent profile followed by a silent phone is like opening a restaurant and leaving the waiter in another state.

What actually affects local visibility?

Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and popularity. Accurate information helps Google understand relevance. Distance depends on the searcher's location and the business location. Reviews and mentions can contribute to popularity, but no agency can pay Google for a guaranteed better local position.

That means a Lagos business may appear differently to a searcher in Yaba and another in Lekki. Rankings are not one permanent chair with your name engraved on it.

FAQs

Is Google Business Profile free in Nigeria?

Google's profile product is free to create and manage for eligible businesses. You may separately choose to pay a professional to help with setup, content, website improvements or ongoing management.

Can an online-only business use a home or virtual address?

Do not invent a customer-facing location. Google's eligibility rules generally exclude online-only businesses, and virtual offices have specific restrictions. Check the current eligibility and service-area guidance for your situation.

Can WTB guarantee the number-one spot on Google Maps?

No. Google says local ranking cannot be bought or requested. WTB can help eligible businesses improve accurate information, relevant website content and the customer journey, but no honest agency can guarantee a particular position.
