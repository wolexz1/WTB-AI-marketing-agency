Skills for the AI age

What AI skills should children learn first?

By WTB Team | Updated July 23, 2026

Not “how to make AI do everything.” The first skills are knowing what to ask, what to doubt and what remains your responsibility.

Build these skills through missions
The short list

Start with questioning, clear instructions, fact checking, privacy, creativity, empathy and reflection. Tool-specific tricks will change; these habits travel with the child.

The child is the capability

A child who can ask better questions and recognise a weak answer will adapt to new tools. A child who only memorises one app’s buttons may be stuck when the buttons move—which they will, because software enjoys rearranging the furniture.

Seven skills worth practising

Questioning

Turn “tell me about this” into a question with a goal, audience and useful constraints.

Verification

Check important answers against trusted sources, teacher notes, calculations or direct observation.

Privacy

Recognise personal data and know when not to upload it or place it in a prompt.

Creative direction

Use AI for options, then make human choices about story, design, tone and meaning.

Bias awareness

Ask whose perspective is missing and whether an answer stereotypes people or places.

Communication

Explain what the tool produced, what the child changed and what still needs checking.

Reflection

Notice when AI helped, when it distracted and what the child can now do alone.

Human judgement

Know when the right next step is a parent, teacher or trusted expert—not another prompt.

A simple weekly practice

  1. Choose one real question from school, home or the child’s interest.
  2. Write the child’s first answer before asking AI.
  3. Compare the two answers and circle anything uncertain.
  4. Verify one claim and improve one explanation.
  5. Save the child’s reflection, not just the AI output.

UNICEF’s child-centred guidance includes preparing and skilling children for present and future AI developments while protecting safety, privacy and wellbeing. Read the recommendations.
